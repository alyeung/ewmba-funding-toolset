# GA Funding Lessons Learned (Summer 2026 Round)

Based on an analysis of the Summer 2026 Graduate Assembly (GA) funding recommendations and decisions in [GRSO and Independent Organizer Funding.xlsx](file:///Users/allanyeung/Documents/ewmba-fund/GRSO%20and%20Independent%20Organizer%20Funding.xlsx), here are the key lessons learned for drafting and submitting successful funding proposals.

---

## 1. Multiple Budget Requests & Multiple Large Projects

*   **Ability to Submit Multiple Budgets:** Organizations **can** apply for funding for multiple distinct events in a single round.
*   **Submission Format:** The Funding Officer recommends putting all events/budgets into **one single Large-Project funding application** rather than submitting separate, multiple applications.
    *   *Example:* Connor Tamor (Transfers at Berkeley Law) submitted 4 separate Large-Project applications. The Funding Officer noted:
        > *"In the future, when applying for multiple large projects, please put them all in the same Large-Project funding application."*
*   **Spending Flexibility:** When multiple budgets are approved under a group cap, the allocated funds do not have to be spent exactly as allocated to specific individual events. The total approved amount can be spent **freely across any of the approved projects** (within the limits of the individual budgets submitted).
    *   *Example Note:* 
        > *"The allocations here do not mean that you are required to spend the $1500 on the first two projects. It can be spent freely (within your submitted budgets) on any of the four projects."*

---

## 2. Round-Specific Funding Caps

*   **Practical Capping ($1,500):** Although the official semester cap for Large-Project Funding is $2,000, in practice, the Funding Committee regularly caps the maximum recommended amount at **$1,500 per group** during high-demand rounds.
    *   *Impacted Groups:* This cap was applied to the Haas Evening and Weekend MBA Association, 1G at Haas, Chinese Graduate and Postdoctoral Scholars Association, BAVRD, and others.
*   **Action Item:** Design budgets with the understanding that requests for the full $2,000 will likely be reduced to $1,500. Essential items should be prioritized in case of cuts.

---

## 3. Strict Event Windowing & Deadlines

*   **Round Alignment:** Events must occur strictly within the dates specified for the funding round.
    *   *Example:* The Organization of Jewish Graduate Students had a portion of their request cut because *"The first dinner falls outside of the funding round."*
*   **Fall vs. Summer Round:** Large-project events occurring in the fall semester must be applied for in the **September round**, not the Summer round, unless they fall before the September decisions are announced (September 10).
    *   *Example:* Vietnamese American Law Students (VALS) applied for fall events in the Summer round and were told:
        > *"Large projects in the fall semester should be applied for in the September funding round... your last event doesn't qualify as a large project, since it doesn't meet the criteria of costing more than $500."*
*   **Large-Project Threshold:** Every individual event included in a Large-Project request must independently meet the criteria of costing **more than $500**. If an event costs less than $500, it does not qualify.

---

## 4. Reclassifying for Success

*   **Category Adjustments:** The Funding Officer may reclassify a submission (e.g., interpreting parts of a request as a Large Project and other parts as Continuing Funding) to help a group maximize the amount they receive.
    *   *Example:* The Mixology Club's request of $1,250 was split into $750 for a Large Project in August and $500 for Continuing Funding for the other events.

---

## 5. Compliance Reminders

*   **Banned Items Check:** Ensure budgets contain no forbidden items. The Funding Officer explicitly warns groups about this during evaluations.
    *   *Example Note for 1G at Haas:*
        > *"Note also that as per the funding guide restrictions, you are forbidden from spending funds on items with logos and disposable, non-compostable cutlery."*
